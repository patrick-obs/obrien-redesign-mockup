Static Shelving

shelv·ing (n.)Whatever was here when you moved in.Redefined: Sized for what you store, rated for what it weighs.

Static shelving keeps storage simple and affordable, with 4-post and case-style shelving, pallet rack, cantilever library shelving and rack, and industrial steel shelving to organize any space.

Static shelving stays in one place and organizes everything from files and archives to pallets and oversized items. It sits right on the floor with no rails or carriages, so it's one of the quickest and easiest storage upgrades for most facilities.

4-post and case-style shelving can be customized with dividers, drawers, and doors for files, records, and general storage. Pallet rack handles bulky, heavy loads. Cantilever library shelving is a popular choice for libraries, schools, and offices, while cantilever rack stores long items like lumber, pipe, and tubing. Industrial steel shelving is built for heavy-duty parts and supplies.

Most projects combine more than one type. Many 4-post systems can also be mounted on carriages later and converted to high-density mobile storage when you need more space, so we design with that upgrade in mind from the start.

Built-in woodwork or modular steel?

Built-ins look good in a lobby, but storage rooms change. Steel shelving adjusts as your needs change, moves when you move, and can go onto mobile carriages later. For working storage, modular steel usually costs less over its life than custom carpentry.

Frequently asked questions

What types of static shelving does O'Brien Systems offer?

We offer 4-post and case-style shelving for files, records, and general storage; pallet rack for exceptionally large and heavy palletized loads; cantilever shelving suited to education and corporate settings; and industrial metal shelving for parts and supplies. Most projects combine more than one type, and the right mix is determined during a free site assessment.

How much weight can static shelving hold?

Capacity depends on the shelving type, shelf size, and configuration. Industrial metal shelving on this line holds up to 600 pounds per shelf, while pallet rack is engineered for much heavier palletized loads. We size shelf gauge, spans, and uprights to what you actually store and confirm loads during the site assessment.

Can static shelving be reconfigured or moved later?

Yes. 4-post and case-style systems are designed to be disassembled and reassembled, so shelving can move with a department or be reconfigured as needs change. We perform teardown, relocation, and reinstallation as a service, including for shelving we did not originally supply.

Can static shelving be converted to high-density mobile storage later?

Often, yes. Many 4-post shelving systems can be mounted on mobile carriages down the road, which lets you buy shelving now and convert it to high-density mobile storage when space runs out. Planning for that upgrade path at initial purchase keeps the option open.

What determines the cost of a shelving project?

The main factors are shelving type and gauge, the number of sections, height and shelf count, accessories such as dividers and doors, and installation conditions. Because static shelving is modular, projects can be phased, starting with the highest-need areas and adding sections later.

Is it cheaper to build shelves or buy them?

For working storage, buying modular steel shelving is usually the better value. It goes in faster than custom carpentry, adjusts as your needs change, and can be moved or converted to mobile storage later. Built-in woodwork makes sense for finished spaces where looks come first.

Can we start with static shelving and go mobile later?

Yes. We often lay out four-post shelving so it can move onto carriages when space runs out, which can spread the cost over two budget years.
