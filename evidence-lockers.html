Evidence Lockers

Chain of custody starts at the deposit door. Pass-through evidence lockers create a secure, one-way transfer from officer to evidence custodian.

A pass-through evidence locker has two sides. Officers deposit evidence through the front, and the door locks behind them. Only the evidence custodian can open the compartment from the rear. With electronic locks, every deposit and retrieval is logged with a timestamp, creating a complete audit trail.

We design evidence intake as a complete system: a mix of locker sizes for phones, firearms, and bulky items, refrigerated compartments for biological evidence, and the high-density mobile storage and bin storage where evidence is kept long-term.

Police departments across the Philadelphia region use evidence intake rooms we've designed, and our own crews handle installation. The same chain-of-custody approach works for crime labs, internal affairs, and any operation that needs to document who handled what, and when.

Frequently asked questions

What is an evidence locker?

A secure compartment where an officer deposits evidence so the chain of custody is never broken. Once a door closes, only the evidence custodian can open it again.

Pass-through or non-pass-through?

Pass-through lockers sit in a wall between the officer side and the evidence room: the officer loads from the front and the custodian unloads from the secure back. Non-pass-through lockers load and unload from the same side and suit smaller departments or rooms without a shared wall.

Where do police departments store evidence?

In a secure property and evidence room with controlled access, usually behind temporary lockers where officers drop items. Long-term storage is often high-density shelving, drug and weapons cages, and refrigerated lockers for biological evidence.
