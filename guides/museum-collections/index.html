Museum and collections storage

Preserving history for the ages

Improved preservation. Improved security. Improved access.

Better ways to store and preserve

Security, preservation, and access: the right storage keeps a collection safe, easy to reach, and ready to grow. Every project starts with an on-site needs analysis. We review your object inventory, look at your space and security needs, and design storage that fits your collection, from a single cabinet to a full collections storage facility.

We design, supply, and install, and our own crews handle every installation throughout the Philadelphia region and beyond. We work alongside your registrars, conservators, architects, and engineers so the storage supports your conservation standards from the first drawing.

Storage for every part of the collection

Preservation cabinets

Steel museum storage cabinets with removable trays and drawers for herbarium sheets, fossils, insects, archaeology, and archival collections. Gasketed doors help protect against dust and pests and maintain a stable microclimate, and collection-safe finishes won’t off-gas onto collections.

Visible storage

Glass-front cabinets over drawers put study collections on view while keeping them protected, so visitors and researchers can see objects without handling them.

Standard and specialty drawers

Flat files and full-extension drawers for maps, prints, photographs, textiles, and works on paper. Full-extension drawers let you lift an object straight out without sliding or rubbing.

High-density mobile storage

Shelving, cabinets, and racks mounted on carriages that glide along floor rails, so only the aisle you need is open. The same room can hold up to twice the collection, and systems can be locked closed for added security.

Art racks and screens

Pull-out and lateral mesh screens for framed works and hanging textiles. Each piece hangs on its own hooks, face out and visible, with nothing leaning or stacked.

Framed art storage bins

Divided bins for framed and unframed works stored upright, each slot padded and sized to the piece, with roll-up doors that help protect against dust and light.

Wall-mounted art screens

Fixed mesh panels mounted to a wall hold framed works, plaques, and lighter sculpture on ledges and hooks, turning wall space in a vault or workroom into storage.

Solander box shelving

4-post shelving sized to archival and solander boxes, with shallow shelves so every box slides out on its own and nothing is stacked on top of another.

Textile racks

Rolled storage for rugs, quilts, flags, and other large textiles, with each roll supported on its own tube. Deep rolled pieces come out without unloading their neighbors.

Costume and hanging storage

Garment rods, shelves, and drawers combined in one system for historical clothing, costumes, and accessories, with room for padded hangers and boxed pieces side by side.

Wide-span and bulk shelving

Heavy-duty open shelving for furniture, sculpture, geological specimens, animal mounts, and other large or irregular objects, with padded decks and room to move pieces safely.

Mobile object racks

Open racks on carriages that glide along floor rails for sculpture, ceramics, and boxed objects, so large, heavy pieces get the same space savings as high-density mobile storage.

Wall E-track systems

Steel track rails anchored to the wall, with straps that secure crates, pallets, framed works, and sculpture in place during storage, moves, and installation.

Weapons and long objects

Wall and rack systems that hold staffs, spears, firearms, and other long objects upright and separated, each in its own padded cradle.

Library and archive shelving

Cantilever library shelving, high-density mobile storage, and static shelving for a museum library, curatorial files, catalogs, and archival records, kept alongside the collection they document.

Collections workrooms

Worktables, flat files, and cabinets for processing, study, and conservation rooms, designed with the storage so objects move a short, safe distance.
